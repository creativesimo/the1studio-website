How to use Image Resizer

  1. Drop images or a folder into Image Resizer.
  2. Choose File size, Dimensions or Percentage, then set your limit.
  3. Click Resize and inspect the saved copies in Results or the Resized folder.

Set a maximum file size, such as 500 KB

Give each image a size limit for an upload form or email attachment. A smaller file-size target can require lower quality or smaller dimensions; results show the actual size and dimensions of every saved copy.

Resize by dimensions or percentage

Choose a maximum longest edge for a website or reduce images by a percentage. Optional combined constraints help when you need to meet both a pixel limit and a file-size limit.

Process folders in one batch

Add images or folders, including their subfolders. Duplicate selections and generated Resized folders are skipped. Optional numbered filenames keep the exported batch tidy.

Save copies and keep your originals

Output goes to Resized folders. Existing outputs are protected with numbered filenames. Results show saved names, byte sizes and dimensions, with a way to open the output folder.

Questions about Image Resizer

Can I resize multiple images to under 500 KB?

Yes. Choose the file-size mode and enter 500 KB. The app processes each image separately and reports files that cannot meet the selected constraints instead of silently replacing your originals.

Which image formats can I save?

Mac supports JPEG, PNG, HEIC and TIFF, subject to the encoders available on that Mac. Windows supports JPEG, PNG and TIFF; it does not export HEIC. WebP and AVIF output belong to Image Optimizer, not Image Resizer.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. Resizing takes place on your computer. Optional privacy filtering removes location and camera details from exported copies; it is not a guarantee of complete anonymization.

The Mac App Store and Microsoft Store releases are coming soon. The planned offer is a 7-day free trial followed by a one-time $4.99 purchase. There is no subscription.
